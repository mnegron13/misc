The plan

The program runs across four levels, and not everyone needs all four.

Each level is earned by doing real work that holds up to review, so it reflects what a person can really do. People climb only as far as their role needs. This walks through what's coming for your teams, and the last slide covers where we'll need you.

Move through the slides for the detail on each level, or tap a card to jump straight to one.

1
Level 1 · AI User One self-paced WorkRamp path

Everyone on your team can use AI confidently in daily work, and they can tell when its output is solid and fix it when it isn't.

Who it's forEveryone in Client Ops
Reaches the fieldBy Aug 30, 2026
DueSep 30, 2026

What it teaches

This level builds on AI Fluency and AI Tools. On top of those, people take a short course on tokens and right-sizing their AI use, then pass a learning check where they apply what they've learned. By the end they can spot weak output, fix it, send work to the right tool, and know where the final call is still theirs.

How it's earned

  • They finish both earlier paths, AI Fluency and AI Tools, which carry over if those are already done.
  • They take a short course on tokens and right-sizing their AI use.
  • They pass a learning check where they apply what they've learned.

We'll track the learning-check pass rate and completion across the team.

2
Level 2 · AI Builder, personal WorkRamp path plus Champion build sessions

Someone on your team has built a personal tool, a Skill or a Dust agent, that runs on its own and saves them time.

Who it's forAll individual contributors
Reaches the fieldBy Oct 1, 2026
DueNov 1, 2026

What it teaches

This is the step from using AI to building with it. People pick between a Skill and a Dust agent, tighten a rough first version until it runs reliably, measure the time it saves, and package it to submit.

How it's earned

  • They publish a working Skill or Dust agent.
  • They submit the problem it solves, a demo on real input, the time it saves, and a registry entry.
  • A Champion scores it against the rubric, and a 30-day check confirms it's still in use.

We'll track certified builds by team and the hours saved that hold up after the 30-day check.

3
Level 3 · AI Builder, team Async primer plus a live cohort with AI Ops

A lead or Champion on your team has built a tool the team actually uses.

Who it's forLevel 2 holders, mainly leaders and Champions
Reaches the fieldFirst cohort by Nov 2026provisional
DueEnd of Jan 2027provisional

What it teaches

This is where people build something the whole team relies on. They set up the tools to build with, which are Claude Code and GitHub, work from a shared foundation the AI Ops team provides, and help the team start using what they make.

How it's earned

  • They build a team tool on that shared foundation.
  • A teammate demos it live, and the usage data shows at least three people using it who didn't build it.
  • They add it to the team library and give a short teach-back at AI Show and Tell.

We'll track certified team tools whose adoption shows up in the usage data.

4
Level 4 · Org Builder Still being scoped · design happens in H2

The aim is for a leader to set up, govern, and coach an agentic function for their team.

Who it's forLeaders and AI Ops
Reaches the fieldFeb 2027provisional
DueEnd of Mar 2027provisional

What it teaches

This level isn't finalized. We're still designing it with the AI Ops team, and it depends on infrastructure that isn't in place yet. Our current thinking is that a leader sets up how a whole team works with AI day to day, guides how other people build, and coaches them using live data.

How it might be earned

  • Set up a working queue or set of rules for the team.
  • Review other people's team builds as a validator.
  • Coach the team using live performance data.

We'll define how success is measured during H2 design.

Your part

This is where leaders make the difference.

Enablement can build this program and run it, but it can't create the requirement, protect people's time, or set the expectation. Those three things sit with you, and they're what decide whether the program lands.

01

Tell your teams this matters and has leadership behind it, and keep saying it as the program rolls out.

02

Protect the time

Treat the work as part of the job, and hold real time open for people to do it.

03

Hold teams to finishing

Expect people to finish, and hold your Champions to running their sessions.

04

Back the rewards

There's a $1,000 pool, up to $950, to recognize ICs with a small reward, weighted so the deeper work earns the most.
