The case for it

Why a certification program?

The better people get with AI, the more useful tools they build, and those are what scale. So we certify the skills that make it possible.

Say the goal was to sell 1,000 pieces of fried chicken 🍗

🔪 knife skills 🔥 the fryer 🥣 breading

You'd teach the skills first. The output follows.

It's the same with AI. Before people can build tools that save real hours, they need a handful of core skills. The certification teaches and confirms them:

🎯Prompting well and giving the right context
✏️Iterating until the output is right
🎚️Picking the right model and level of reasoning
🪙Understanding tokens and right-sizing requests
🔍Judging and fixing what AI gives back
🧩Building reusable Skills and agents
The path

So, what's the certification path?

Four levels, delivered in WorkRamp from August through March. Everyone starts at Level 1 and moves up only as far as their role needs. Each level is earned by building something real and having it checked.

What it teaches

Most of this is already done. Nobody retakes AI Fluency or AI Tools; those records carry over. Level 1 formalizes them, then adds one net-new course on AI tokens and reasoning effort and a final learning check, about 30 minutes for anyone who finished the earlier paths. By the end they can spot weak output, fix it, route work to the right tool, and know where the final call is theirs.

How it's earned

  • They finish both earlier paths, AI Fluency and AI Tools, which carry over if those are already done.
  • They take one net-new course on AI tokens and reasoning effort.
  • They pass a learning check where they apply what they've learned.

We'll track the learning-check pass rate and completion across the team.

2
Level 2 · AI Builder, personal WorkRamp path plus Champion build sessions

Someone on your team has built a personal tool, a Skill or a Dust agent, that runs on its own and saves them time.

Who it's forEveryone in Client Ops
Reaches the fieldBy Oct 1, 2026
DueEnd of Nov 2026

What it teaches

This is the step from using AI to building with it. People pick between a Skill and a Dust agent, tighten a rough first version until it runs reliably, measure the time it saves, and package it to submit.

How it's earned

  • They publish a working Skill or Dust agent.
  • They submit the problem it solves, a demo on real input, the time it saves, and a registry entry.
  • A Champion scores it against the rubric, and a 30-day check confirms it's still in use.

We'll track certified builds by team and the hours saved that hold up after the 30-day check.
